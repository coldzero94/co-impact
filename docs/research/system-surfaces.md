# Research: spend one alert well, keep every other surface quiet

Researched 2026-11-15. Goal: find how Impact can serve people better in the app and on the iOS surfaces beyond it, given no backend or push, no dependence on Apple Intelligence, a closed action catalog, and the rule that fewer cards and fewer interruptions beat more. This extends [award-winning-apps.md](award-winning-apps.md), [UX.md → System surfaces](../UX.md#system-surfaces), and [PRODUCT.md → Interruptions](../PRODUCT.md#interruptions) instead of repeating them. Screens were reviewed from simulator screenshots with synthetic data.

The owner's decisions on these findings (2026-11-15) live in [PRODUCT.md → Interruptions](../PRODUCT.md#interruptions), [UX.md → System surfaces](../UX.md#system-surfaces), and [ROADMAP.md](../ROADMAP.md); the early-tap rule goes into PRODUCT.md → Completion semantics with its code. The rest of the ranked proposals are open.

Source labels: **SDK-verified** = read in the iOS 27.0 SDK of Xcode 27.0 (`.swiftinterface` files, their doc comments, or headers). **Apple doc** = developer.apple.com documentation, the HIG, or a WWDC session. **Apple forum** = an Apple engineer's reply on the Developer Forums. **Third-party** = everything else; peer-reviewed studies and vendor surveys are marked.

Impact gains most by getting one moment right, not by adding surfaces. iOS 26 lets an app with no server schedule a Live Activity for a card's Now phase. But **every scheduled start must alert, nothing after the start can be scheduled, and no system surface can open Zoom, Maps, or a phone call directly**: each action passes through Impact for one hop. The plan in UX.md still works under three conditions. The Live Activity's start alert must be the card's only alert. Its content must stay correct without updates, which means fixed times rendered by system timers. And Impact must end it whenever the app gets to run. Everything else should be passive. A Lock Screen widget and Smart Stack entries rise only from the Soon phase. StandBy and CarPlay reuse the small widget, the Watch gets the Live Activity automatically, and Siri, Spotlight, and the Action button use App Shortcuts that need no Apple Intelligence. Comparable apps confirm the pattern (Luma: one countdown, one action chosen by event type) and its main risk: activities that outlive their event, which is why BusyCal ships none. The research on lateness and notifications favors one well-timed cue and less friction at the start. The research on trust finds that people judge a missed event more harshly than an extra one, so Impact should let people see what it left out rather than loosen selection. The current screens are structurally sound, but one rule breaks the promise: tapping Directions the evening before marks the card done and removes it from Next up, so the morning cue that card needed disappears.

## A scheduled Live Activity needs no server, but it always alerts and cannot end itself

The scheduled start is real and unchanged in iOS 27. `Activity.request(attributes:content:pushType:style:alertConfiguration:start:)` is iOS 26.0+, and the iOS 27 ActivityKit interface carries no newer annotation. The request returns an activity in the new `.pending` state (SDK-verified: ActivityKit). The system starts it at the given date "even if the app is in the background" ([Apple doc: request(…start:)](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/activitykit/activity/request(attributes:content:pushtype:style:alertconfiguration:start:))). Three constraints come with it. First, **`alertConfiguration` is not optional, and `AlertSound` offers only `.default` and `.named(_:)`**: there is no silent option, and no interruption level of any kind (SDK-verified: ActivityKit). On iPhone the alert shows as the expanded Dynamic Island, or as a Lock Screen banner on devices without one, and by default it lights up the screen and plays the notification sound ([Apple doc: Displaying live data](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/activitykit/displaying-live-data-with-live-activities); [Apple doc: HIG Live Activities](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/live-activities)). Second, scheduled activities count toward an undocumented limit on simultaneous activities. Third, **there is no API to schedule an update or an end**: `update`'s `timestamp` only orders updates, and `end` takes only a dismissal policy that applies after the activity has ended (SDK-verified: ActivityKit). One developer reports that a scheduled start creates a second activity instead of updating the first, and that `staleDate` took effect a minute or two late ([third-party: Developer Forums 851732](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/851732)). A merged Expo change observed, on a simulator only, that ending a pending activity cancels its start ([third-party: expo/expo#52921](https://github.com/expo/expo/pull/52921)).

For Impact this turns "preferred carrier" in PRODUCT.md into the only safe design: **the Live Activity's start alert is the card's one alert, and the local notification is strictly a fallback, chosen when the activity is scheduled**. `request` throws `denied`, `globalMaximumExceeded`, or `targetMaximumExceeded` at once (SDK-verified: ActivityKit), so Impact knows at analysis time whether to schedule a notification instead. The HIG separately forbids sending notifications "alongside Live Activities for the same updates" ([Apple doc: HIG Live Activities](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/live-activities)). Because pending activities use up the shared limit and each one alerts, Impact should keep at most the next one or two cards pending and re-plan on every run. One gap in Impact's docs matters: `AlertConfiguration` has no interruption level, so PRODUCT.md's "`timeSensitive` for meeting start and leave-by" applies only to the fallback notification. **Whether the Live Activity's start alert gets through a Focus or the silent switch is undocumented.** If it does not, a Time Sensitive notification is the more reliable carrier for a meeting, so this is the first device test step 9 needs.

The content has to stay right without updates. The limits are 4 KB of data, eight hours active, and up to four more hours on the Lock Screen ([Apple doc: Displaying live data](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/activitykit/displaying-live-data-with-live-activities)). Within them, system-rendered views count on their own: `Text(timerInterval:countsDown:)`, `Text(_:style: .relative)`, and iOS 18's `.timer(countingDownIn:)`. `staleDate` flips `context.isStale` without the app (SDK-verified: SwiftUICore, ActivityKit). So the activity should carry only fixed facts: start time, provider or place, a leave-by computed at analysis time, and a countdown drawn by timer text. After the Now phase, an `isStale` branch shows a neutral "Started 10:00". What remains is the risk users complain about most. **If Impact does not run, the activity cannot end, and it stays in the Dynamic Island for up to eight hours** unless the user swipes it away. Impact can count on two runs. The first is the action itself: Join or Directions brings Impact to the foreground, and since the activity's job is then done, Impact can end it immediately. The second is the next launch, when Apple recommends ending activities that no longer apply. Background refresh is best effort. The same gap applies before the start: a meeting moved or cancelled after scheduling still fires its alert unless Impact runs first. That argues for scheduling only the nearest card, as late as the engine allows.

One configuration also serves every place a Live Activity appears: StandBy, the Apple Watch Smart Stack (iOS 18 and watchOS 11, no watch app needed), the CarPlay Dashboard (iOS 26), and the menu bar of a paired Mac ([Apple doc: Creating custom views](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/activitykit/creating-custom-views-for-live-activities); [Apple doc: WWDC25 What's new in widgets](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/278/)). By default the Watch and CarPlay combine the compact leading and trailing views, so **that pair must read as one unit ("video symbol · 4 min")**. A `.supplementalActivityFamilies([.small])` layout replaces it on both. The HIG asks for no buttons there when people may see it while driving, and in CarPlay buttons do nothing and a tap opens only apps that support CarPlay ([Apple doc: HIG Live Activities](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/live-activities); [Apple doc: Launching your app](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/activitykit/launching-your-app-from-a-live-activity)). The car is therefore glance-only: an in-person card must get the user into Maps on the phone before they drive. On the Watch, the alert shows `AlertConfiguration`'s title and body (SDK-verified: ActivityKit doc comment), so that text is a privacy surface too. iOS 27 adds a Dynamic Island in landscape and `isDynamicIslandLimitedInWidth` (SDK-verified: WidgetKit, iOS 27.0). Compact and minimal views must then fit a 28-pt circle, and a leading-only compact view is allowed. iPhone 18 Pro shows two activities at reduced width ([Apple doc: isDynamicIslandLimitedInWidth](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/swiftui/environmentvalues/isdynamicislandlimitedinwidth); [Apple doc: Displaying live data](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/activitykit/displaying-live-data-with-live-activities)). The minimal view should show remaining time, not a logo ([Apple doc: HIG Live Activities](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/live-activities)).

## Every action passes through Impact, and quiet surfaces carry the rest

No documented path lets a system surface open a third-party meeting URL, Maps, or `tel:` by itself. `Link` and `widgetURL` "activate the containing app" with the URL ([Apple doc: Linking to app scenes](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/widgetkit/linking-to-specific-app-scenes-from-your-widget-or-live-activity)). A `LiveActivityIntent` runs in the background and "it is not possible to open an app" with it ([Apple forum: thread 812949](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/812949)). `OpenURLIntent` is documented for the app's own universal links ([Apple doc: OpenURLIntent](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/appintents/openurlintent)), and Impact has none. Notification actions carry no URL, only options such as `.foreground` (SDK-verified: UserNotifications). Widget and Live Activity buttons are inactive on a locked device ([Apple doc: Adding interactivity](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/widgetkit/adding-interactivity-to-widgets-and-live-activities)), and one developer reports that a `Link` failed from the Lock Screen presentation but worked in the expanded Dynamic Island ([third-party: Developer Forums 812949](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/812949)). "One tap" therefore means one user tap plus a brief flash of Impact. That hop is useful: it is the run in which Impact records the Impact, learns whether the target opened (`OpenURLAction` reports this; ARCHITECTURE.md → Platform facts), and ends the Live Activity. The deep-link path must open the target before drawing any Home UI. The Impact moment already waits for the user's return.

| Surface | Works with no server | Limit that shapes Impact's use |
|---------|----------------------|--------------------------------|
| Live Activity (Dynamic Island, Lock Screen, StandBy, Watch, CarPlay) | Scheduled start, timer text, `staleDate` | Start always alerts; no scheduled update or end; buttons dead when locked and in CarPlay |
| Widgets (Home Screen, Lock Screen, StandBy, CarPlay) | Timeline entries, live timer text, Smart Stack relevance | 40–70 reloads a day; actions open Impact first |
| Controls (Control Center, Lock Screen, Action button) | A button that brings Impact forward | Value refreshes only on reload; the Lock Screen shows only a symbol |
| App Shortcuts (Siri, Spotlight, Action button) | Fixed phrases, spoken dialog, snippet views | Phrases must contain the app name |
| Local notifications | `timeSensitive` through Focus and the summary | Not through the silent switch; about 64 pending |
| Focus filter | Filter cards and silence non-matching notifications | Set up in Settings; needs an App Intents extension |
| AlarmKit | Fixed-time alarm through silent mode and Focus | Separate permission; Apple says it does not replace Time Sensitive notifications |

Widgets are the main quiet surface. Timer and relative text update without reloads. A frequently viewed widget gets roughly 40 to 70 reloads a day, entries should be at least five minutes apart, and reloads made while the app is in the foreground are free ([Apple doc: Keeping a widget up to date](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/widgetkit/keeping-a-widget-up-to-date)). On iPhone, **Smart Stack rotation uses each timeline entry's relevance score and duration plus donated `PredictableIntent`s, not the `relevance()` callback** ([Apple doc: Smart Stacks](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/widgetkit/widget-suggestions-in-smart-stacks)), although another Apple page says the opposite. With entries at phase boundaries and a score above zero only from Soon until the start or leave-by, the widget covers the Soon phase silently and the Live Activity covers Now with its one alert. That split exists because a scheduled start cannot be silent. In tinted, clear, and vibrant rendering the system strips color ([Apple doc: Accented rendering and Liquid Glass](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/widgetkit/optimizing-your-widget-for-accented-rendering-mode-and-liquid-glass)), so provenance must be carried by symbols and words, as UX.md already requires. A `systemSmall` widget with a removable background also serves StandBy and CarPlay ([Apple doc: HIG Widgets](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/widgets)). The HIG tells Lock Screen widgets not to act as launchers, which fits a read-only "Zoom 10:00 · in 12 min". WidgetKit push exists from iOS 26 but needs APNs (SDK-verified: WidgetKit), so it is out of scope.

Controls and App Shortcuts reach the Action button and Siri without Apple Intelligence. A `ControlWidgetButton` (iOS 18) whose intent brings Impact forward, through `OpenIntent` or iOS 26's `supportedModes` (`openAppWhenRun` is deprecated in 26), can sit in Control Center, on the Lock Screen, or on the Action button (SDK-verified: SwiftUI, AppIntents; [Apple doc: Creating controls](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/widgetkit/creating-controls-to-perform-actions-across-the-system)). A control's value refreshes only when it is reloaded or used, so it should show a clock time, not a countdown. App Shortcuts are "available immediately when installation finishes" through Siri, Spotlight, the Shortcuts app, and the Action button. Their phrases must include the app name, and their dialog must carry the full answer for audio-only devices ([Apple doc: HIG App Shortcuts](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/app-shortcuts)). iOS 27 adds App Schemas, including a calendar domain with create, update, and delete event intents (SDK-verified: AppIntents). They target Apple Intelligence Siri and the calendar of record. Impact is neither, so it should skip them.

The remaining channels are narrower. A Time Sensitive notification breaks through Focus and the scheduled summary but **not the Ring/Silent switch**, and is meant for events "happening now or within an hour". The system explains it the first time and later offers to turn it off ([Apple doc: HIG Managing notifications](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/managing-notifications)). An app keeps at most about 64 pending requests ([Apple forum: thread 811171](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/811171)), far more than one alert per card needs. Apple documents no developer control over Apple Intelligence summaries or Priority Notifications. Press reports say Time Sensitive items still rise to the top ([third-party: Courier](https://www.courier.com/blog/developer-guide-to-ios-26-priority-notifications)). A Focus filter (`SetFocusFilterIntent` plus `filterCriteria`, SDK-verified) can silence work-calendar alerts in a Personal Focus ([Apple doc: WWDC22 Meet Focus filters](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2022/10121/)), but people set it up in Settings, so it belongs to a later step. AlarmKit (iOS 26, SDK-verified) overrides silent mode and Focus and needs its own permission and an `NSAlarmKitUsageDescription`. Apple calls alarms "not a replacement for … critical alerts or time-sensitive notifications" ([Apple doc: WWDC25 AlarmKit](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/230/)), and no AlarmKit entitlement exists ([Apple forum: thread 797950](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/797950)). A leave-by is computed and shifts with traffic. AlarmKit therefore fits only an explicit per-card opt-in, which would be a new catalog action under Hard rule 3. On every surface, only calendar and computed facts appear; AI "Suggested" steps stay in card detail.

## Comparable apps converge on one countdown and one action, and users punish activities that outlive the event

The closest analog is Luma. Its event Live Activity starts an hour before. It shows a countdown that turns into a LIVE badge, the venue or the platform, and **one action chosen by event type: Directions, Join, or Open Ticket**. It runs only for events the user registered for or hosts, not mere invitations ([third-party: Luma help](https://help.luma.com/p/live-activities)). Calendar apps split. Fantastical runs one automatic activity that walks through the day, offers a per-calendar opt-out, and relies on push for timely updates ([third-party: Fantastical help](https://flexibits.com/fantastical-ios/help/live-activities)). BusyCal ships no Live Activity because they "can stop updating, can get stuck on the Lock Screen", and offers Lock Screen widgets instead ([third-party: BusyCal FAQ](https://www.busymac.com/docs/faqs/busycal-ios-live-activities)). Google Calendar has Lock Screen widgets and no Live Activity was found, but in August 2026 added a prominent "Join video call" button that recognizes Teams, Zoom, and Webex links ([third-party: Google Workspace Updates](https://workspaceupdates.googleblog.com/2026/08/improving-google-calendars-interoperability-with-third-party-video-conferencing-solutions.html)). Microsoft cancelled its Teams meeting Live Activity in October 2025 after several delays ([third-party: roadmap archive](https://app.cloudscout.one/evergreen-item/412616)). Small apps from 2025–26 already ship Impact's mechanics. Next Meeting Live has a "depart in / starts in" dual countdown and claims no servers ([third-party: App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/next-meeting-live/id6754576257)). Proximum has widget Join buttons, StandBy, and opt-in leave alerts, and despite its no-server marketing it keeps a token "until the last scheduled update" ([third-party: App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id6787688495)). Nigh sends four departure alerts per trip and puts its Live Activity behind a subscription ([third-party: App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id6761758206)). The mechanics are crowded. What sets Impact apart is selection (only events that need the user), an activity that is correct without a server by design, and restraint.

The complaints repeat. Activities stop updating or stay after the event: BusyCal's reason above, a Fantastical fix for an island that "might continue showing after the end of an event" ([third-party: App Store release notes](https://apps.apple.com/np/app/id718043190?l=en-gb)), and Uber's beta, which pushed Uber onto a backend. Uber's guidance holds anyway: the surface is "only glanced at for a few seconds", show time first, and **its Live Activity cut driver cancellations by 2.26% and rider cancellations by 2.13%** ([third-party: Uber Engineering](https://www.uber.com/blog/live-activity-on-ios/)). Duplicate alerts are the second complaint: people get one banner from Outlook and one from Teams per meeting and cannot silence just one ([third-party: Microsoft Tech Community](https://techcommunity.microsoft.com/discussions/microsoftteams/teams---disable-meeting-notifications/1073722)). A promotional comparison page reports that Apple Wallet hides its own flight activity when the airline has one ([third-party: Flighty](https://flighty.com/compare/apple-wallet)), a precedent for deferring to the other source. The third complaint is distrust of Apple Calendar's Time to Leave after wrong-mode or inflated estimates, mostly in reports from before iOS 17 ([third-party: Apple Community](https://discussions-cn-prz.apple.com/thread/254122238)). Showing the basis of a leave-by ("18 min by car, from your location at 13:40") addresses that and follows Hard rule 2. Bliro offers "with invitees" and "with external invitees" filters and never fires for cancelled events ([third-party: Bliro help](https://help.bliro.io/en/articles/16360398-meeting-notifications-and-live-activities-bliro-mobile-app)), echoing Luma's registered-only rule. Strict selection is what people get from the apps they like.

## The research favors one well-timed cue and visible misses over more cards

Lateness has a measurable cost. In an experiment with 32 groups, meetings started 10 minutes late were less satisfying and produced less solution-focused talk ([third-party, peer-reviewed: Lehmann-Willenbrock & Allen 2017](https://digitalcommons.unomaha.edu/psychfacpub/203)). People from high power-distance, collectivist cultures judge lateness more harshly ([third-party, peer-reviewed: cross-cultural study 2024](https://www.citedrive.com/en/discovery/late-again-a-cross-cultural-comparison-of-meeting-lateness-frustration-by-punctual-attendees)), which matters for Impact's Korean users. Friction at the start of online meetings is real, though measured only by vendors: about 25% of meetings start late through technical or user error ([third-party, vendor survey: Pexip](https://www.pexip.com/blog/9-frustrating-barriers-to-joining-video-meetings)). For appointments, forgetting is the most reported reason for no-shows, and reminders raise attendance ([third-party, peer-reviewed: PMC3419880](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3419880/)). Long lead times predict no-shows ([third-party, peer-reviewed: PubMed 29482948](https://www.pubmed.gov/29482948/)), which supports the evening "Tomorrow morning" preview. Travel misjudgment is a weaker explanation: commuters overstated car commutes by about 1.5 times ([third-party, peer-reviewed: Peer et al. 2014](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:transa:v:69:y:2014:i:c:p:476-494)). Lateness looks more like a missing, early, or buried cue than a bad estimate. Timing matters too. Notifications delivered at task breakpoints cause less frustration and faster resumption ([third-party, peer-reviewed: Iqbal & Bailey 2010](https://www.interruptions.net/literature/Iqbal-TOCHI10.pdf)), and stress peaks at the switch between back-to-back meetings ([third-party: Microsoft WorkLab, N=14](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/worklab/work-trend-index/brain-research)). A T−10 alert that lands inside the previous meeting arrives at the worst moment; the end of that meeting is a better one.

The notification research supports Impact's budget and sharpens it. People receive about 63.5 notifications a day ([third-party, peer-reviewed: Pielot et al. 2014](https://ic.unicamp.br/~oliveira/doc/MHCI2014_An-in-situ-study-of-mobile-phone-notifications.pdf)). Attention to repeated warnings drops sharply with exposure ([third-party, peer-reviewed: Vance et al., MISQ 2018](https://misq.org/tuning-out-security-warnings-a-longitudinal-examination-of-habituation-through-fmri-eye-tracking-and-field-experiments.html)). Batching to three deliveries a day improved well-being, while hourly batching did little ([third-party, peer-reviewed: Fitz et al. 2019](https://scholars.duke.edu/publication/1402953)). Half of smartwatch sessions last under five seconds ([third-party, peer-reviewed: Visuri et al. 2017](https://zhannina.github.io/papers/chi17.pdf)), so each surface gets one fact and at most one action. The budget Impact shares, though, is per event, not per app. Most meetings already carry a calendar alarm, so **Impact's one alert is often the event's second**. The HIG warns that duplicate notifications lead people to turn off all of an app's notifications ([Apple doc: HIG Notifications](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/notifications)). EventKit exposes an event's explicit alarms (`hasAlarms`, `alarms`, `EKAlarm.relativeOffset`) but has no property for Calendar's Time to Leave (SDK-verified: EventKit, checked for this report). Impact can therefore see some duplicates, not all.

On trust, the research qualifies Hard rule 4 rather than overturning it. In a meeting-detection assistant with equal accuracy, **users rated the high-recall version as more accurate and accepted it more than the high-precision one**, because a false positive was cheap to ignore and a miss was not. Setting expectations before use (an accuracy indicator, examples, a control) raised acceptance, but only for the high-precision version ([third-party, peer-reviewed: Kocielnik et al., CHI 2019](https://microsoft.com/en-us/research/uploads/prod/2019/01/chi19_kocielnik_et_al.pdf)). Impact is a high-precision system, so expectation-setting is exactly what helps it. The summary line ("3 of 4 events need you") does part of that job. A "Why no card?" view would do the rest: plain reasons for each excluded event, as in Amershi's G11 ([third-party, peer-reviewed: Amershi et al. 2019](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/publication/guidelines-for-human-ai-interaction/)). Once people see an algorithm err, they abandon it quickly, but letting them adjust its output even slightly reduces that ([third-party, peer-reviewed: Dietvorst et al. 2015](https://faculty.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2016/11/20-Dietvorst-Simmons-Massey-2015.pdf); [Dietvorst et al. 2018](https://marketing.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2020/07/Dietvorst-Overcoming-Algorithm-Aversion.pdf)). Two consequences follow. A stale alert for a cancelled meeting is the visible kind of error that costs trust. And a per-event override is the cheap control that wins it back. P1 limits that override to events that were excluded despite having a signal (declined, muted, a shared calendar), because without a link or place a card has nothing to answer with.

## The screens hold up, except where an early tap erases the next cue

The structure should stay: the accent Next up card with a large countdown and one white primary button, departure-board rows, the summary line as a trust signal, the calm empty state, and a medium-detent detail sheet with one colored action. These match the HIG on sheets and Liquid Glass color ([Apple doc: HIG Sheets](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/sheets); [Apple doc: HIG Color](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/color)) and the glance research above. The most serious problem is behavioral. [PRODUCT.md → Card timeline](../PRODUCT.md#card-timeline) marks a card done when Directions is tapped, and done cards leave Next up. In the 21:00 screenshot, Monday's 08:30 client visit carries a checkmark because its route was previewed that evening, and the empty state's line reads "Next: Tue 10:00 AM · Quarterly planning", skipping it. **A route preview the night before is preparation, not departure**. If it consumes the card, the morning cue disappears for exactly the appointment that needed it. PRODUCT.md does not say whether done also suppresses the Now alert.

The other problems are about order and timing. At 08:00, the first "Later today" row is the 7:30 standup with a checkmark: a past, done event sits above every upcoming one. The summary line says "5 of 6 events need you" while counting it. A hybrid town hall at City Hall shows "12 min" and "Teams link ready · City Hall", because hybrid cards follow the online timeline: anyone going in person gets no leave-by cue. The visual problems are smaller but concrete. The section headers measure about **1.8:1 contrast against the HIG's 4.5:1 minimum** ([Apple doc: HIG Accessibility](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/accessibility)). The likely cause is the extra `.foregroundStyle(.secondary)` on the section header in [HomeView.swift](../../ImpactKit/Sources/HomeFeature/HomeView.swift), inside a header the system already styles as secondary. Next up names the provider two or three times ("Zoom" header, "Zoom link ready", the video symbol), and its header text sits on the lightest part of the gradient at about 4.0:1. The ring shows a bare number with no unit. Its segments vanish as cards expire, so a full ring at 19:00 can mean "all done" or "the rest expired". It borrows the visual language of Activity rings, which the HIG reserves, and a number in a circle reads like a badge ([Apple doc: HIG Activity rings](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/activity-rings)). The evening empty state says the same thing twice ("No events left today" and "Nothing needs you today."), styles a message as an actionable card, and leaves the Next line untappable, against the HIG's advice to give blank screens a next step ([Apple doc: HIG Writing](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/writing)). Detail sheets repeat the raw meeting URL in the notes under the Join button that already opens it.

## Conclusion

The no-server constraint matters less than it looks for a meeting cue, because the cue's content is fixed times, and the system can render those without Impact. The real limit is retraction: Impact cannot take back or end a cue it scheduled unless it gets to run. Every design choice that keeps Impact's surfaces honest therefore reduces how far ahead it commits: only the next card, as late as possible, cleaned up on every run, and especially the run the action itself causes. The mandatory hop through Impact, which looks like a cost, is what makes that cleanup and the Impact count reliable.

Hard rule 4 survives the trust evidence, but its cost moves somewhere else. Users forgive an extra card more readily than a miss, so a high-precision Impact has to make its misses visible and correctable, not rarer by adding cards. The same logic applies to interruptions. The relevant budget is the event's, not the app's, and the user's calendar has usually spent part of it already. Impact earns its one alert by carrying what the calendar's alert cannot: the link or route, one tap away, at a moment chosen around the user's previous meeting.

## Decisions for the owner

- **Titles on the Lock Screen.** May the Live Activity's Lock Screen view, the Lock Screen widget, the control, notifications, and the Watch alert text show meeting titles, or only "Meeting · 10:00" until unlock? The HIG advises an innocuous summary on the Lock Screen.
- **Early taps.** Does a primary action tapped before the Soon phase keep the card in Next up with its morning cue and alert? Does it still record an Impact (D16, outcome metric)? And does a card that becomes done in Soon cancel its pending Live Activity?
- **Live Activity alert versus the Now notification.** Does the Live Activity's start alert replace the planned Now notification entirely, with a notification only when the activity can't be scheduled? If the device test shows the start alert held back by a Focus, which carrier wins for meetings?
- **Yielding to calendar alarms.** On by default, off, or a setting?
- **Overrides in "Why no card?".** May a user show one excluded event anyway? This touches D1 and Hard rule 4.
- **Hybrid timing.** Online by default, in-person by default, or in-person once the user has opened Directions for that series?
- **New surfaces.** Add the "Next up" control and the Directions shortcut to UX.md → System surfaces. Neither changes the catalog.
- **AlarmKit.** Add "Alarm me to leave" to the action catalog (D2), or not?
- **The ring.** Keep it with a label, keep only filled segments, or replace it with a count?

## Claims still unverified

- How a scheduled Live Activity's start alert looks and sounds on iPhone, and whether it gets through Focus and the silent switch. Apple describes alerts for updates, not for scheduled starts. It is also unknown whether `.named` with a silent sound file works.
- Scheduled start accuracy, maximum lead time, the pending-activity limit (vendors cite 5 per app), survival across reboot or app update, whether the eight-hour clock runs from the start, and behavior for a start date in the past.
- That ending a pending activity cancels its start (seen only on an iOS 27.2 simulator), that `staleDate` applies to an activity the app never updated, and the reported one-to-two-minute `staleDate` lag (one forum report).
- How an ended or stale activity that the app never ends presents in the Dynamic Island over hours.
- Whether a `Link` to a third-party https or `tel:` URL in a Live Activity or widget opens the target directly. Also unverified: a reported `Link` failure on the Lock Screen presentation, and reports that `OpenURLIntent` with an https URL opens Safari (iOS 18 betas).
- Whether a Live Activity button running an `AppIntent` with `.foreground(.immediate)` brings Impact forward, and whether buttons in a `.small` Watch layout run on the iPhone.
- Smart Stack rotation from entry relevance on iOS 26 and 27 (two Apple pages disagree), whether an iPhone-only app's widgets appear on the Watch, and what a widget tap does in CarPlay.
- When a control's `currentValue()` is fetched, and whether a Lock Screen control asks for Face ID before running.
- The 64-pending notification limit (an Apple forum reply; current docs give no number), Time Sensitive needing no Apple approval (third-party), how Priority Notifications rank items (third-party only), and what iOS 27's `appEntityIdentifiers` on notifications does.
- Whether default alert times from Calendar settings and Google or Exchange accounts appear in `EKCalendarItem.alarms`.
- AlarmKit's per-app alarm limit and whether an alarm can be rescheduled in place.
- Whether `privacySensitive` or redaction applies to the Live Activity Lock Screen presentation.
- Third-party claims not used above: relaxed Live Activity budgets in iOS 27, an iOS 27 dark-mode tint bug, a wholesale SiriKit deprecation (only domain-level deprecations are in the SDK), Fantastical's activity content, and Teams' activity status (a blog says it shipped; the roadmap says cancelled).
